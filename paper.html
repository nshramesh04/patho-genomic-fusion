Asymmetric Cross-Attention Networks for Multimodal Integration in Pathology

Genomic-Conditioned Visual Search for PR-Status Classification in Breast Cancer

Author
Affiliation

Independent Research

Published

June 15, 2026

Abstract

Accurate progesterone receptor (PR) status is a critical determinant for endocrine therapy eligibility in breast cancer patients. Standard immunohistochemistry (IHC) encounters a clinical ceiling in near-threshold cases, producing significant inter-pathologist variability that existing assays cannot resolve. This work introduces a clinical decision-support system that addresses diagnostic ambiguity by integrating RNA-Seq transcriptomic profiles with whole-slide imaging (WSI). The core architectural challenge—the structural incompatibility between fixed-length molecular vectors and variable-length WSI patch bags (960 to 39,052 patches)—is resolved through an asymmetric cross-attention mechanism. Here, the patient’s molecular fingerprint acts as a query token attending over all N tissue patches at O(N) cost. Complementing this, a Gated-Fusion module incorporates a lightweight Cross-Modal Reliability Estimator, providing a per-patient concordance signal that triggers automated escalation to pathologist review in discordant cases. Validation on 951 TCGA-BRCA patients achieves an ROC-AUC of 0.8482 and PR-AUC of 0.9054. The Cross-Modal Reliability Estimator produces a directionally stable, class-imbalance-robust per-patient signal (Platt-calibrated balanced p = 1.02e-09). Notably, attention behavior shows statistically significant phenotypic separation between PR- and PR+ patients (p = 0.018); this interpretability confirms the model identifies biologically relevant spatial features, rather than relying on spurious correlations.

Keywords

progesterone receptor status, cross-attention, whole-slide imaging, computational pathology, uncertainty quantification, RNA-Seq

1 The Challenge

1.1 Clinical Necessity

PR status determines whether a breast cancer patient is a candidate for endocrine therapies such as tamoxifen and aromatase inhibitors. A misclassification in either direction carries direct clinical cost: false positives expose patients to ineffective hormonal therapy, while false negatives withhold potentially curative treatment. The current gold standard, IHC staining of biopsy tissue, is subject to significant inter-pathologist disagreement, particularly in borderline cases near the positivity threshold. Computational adjudication using high-dimensional transcriptomic and imaging data offers a path to more consistent, evidence-driven classification.

1.2 The Structural Incompatibility Problem

The core engineering challenge is not modeling capacity; it is data structure. A transcriptomic profile is a fixed-length vector of 20,513 RNA-Seq RSEM features in which every patient occupies an identical coordinate space. A digitized whole-slide image, tiled at 20x, yields a variable-length, unordered bag of patch embeddings whose cardinality N ranges from 960 to 39,052 depending on tissue area and scanner footprint. Because tiles are extracted by raster scan, patch order encodes scanner trajectory rather than tissue organization. Standard fusion architectures either reduce the patch bag to a fixed summary, discarding spatial specificity, or impose positional encodings that tie the model to scanner trajectory rather than tissue biology; neither generalizes across acquisition platforms.

1.3 The Slide-Size Confounder

A subtler but consequential problem arises during interpretability analysis. The intuitive metric for quantifying attentional focus, Shannon entropy, is mathematically bounded above by log N. A patient with 960 patches has a theoretical maximum entropy of ≈ 6.87 nats; one with 39,052 patches has a maximum of ≈ 10.57 nats. Two patients with structurally identical attention distributions will therefore differ in measured entropy by up to 3.7 nats purely as a function of slide size, producing a confounded metric rather than a biological signal.

WarningStatistical Pitfall

A metric that is not normalized for slide size will measure size differences between groups, not biological ones. Shannon entropy fails this test for variable-length attention distributions.

1.4 Related Work

Prior approaches address parts of these challenges but each leaves a critical gap.

Attention-based MIL. Ilse et al. [1] established learnable attention pooling as the dominant paradigm for WSI classification. Lu et al. [2] extended this with CLAM. Both operate within the visual modality alone; a patient’s transcriptomic state has no influence over which tissue regions are examined. The structural incompatibility problem is sidestepped entirely by ignoring the genomic modality.

Multimodal fusion. Chen et al. [3] introduced Pathomic Fusion, combining histology and genomic features through Kronecker-product gating. This fusion occurs downstream of independent modality encoding; the genomic signal conditions how an already-compressed visual summary is weighted, not which tissue evidence is gathered. The structural incompatibility is deferred rather than resolved: by the time genomics enters the pipeline, the slide has already been reduced to a fixed-size summary.

Cross-modal transformers. MCAT [4] applies cross-attention between pathway-grouped genomic tokens and patch embeddings, bringing genomic conditioning upstream of compression. However, its pathway decomposition requires predefined gene-pathway annotations, and its patch embeddings use positional encodings that tie the model to a fixed spatial grid, introducing scanner-dependent biases that do not generalize across acquisition platforms. The slide-size confounder is also unaddressed; no normalization-independent interpretability metric is proposed.

Transcriptomics-guided representation learning. TITAN [5] and RNAPath address cross-modal alignment at the encoder level by pretraining patch embeddings to align with RNA-Seq profiles. The present work identifies near-orthogonal visual and genomic embeddings as the root cause of gate direction instability and prescribes a TGSR encoder as the architectural upgrade for v3 (Section 3.4).

2 Contributions

This work addresses all three gaps through the following contributions:

  • Asymmetric cross-attention fusion: a mechanism that uses the patient’s transcriptomic profile to identify diagnostically relevant tissue patches across the entire slide at linear computational cost, without positional encoding, pathway annotation, or pre-aggregation.
  • Cross-Modal Reliability Estimator: a gated fusion layer producing a directionally stable, class-imbalance-robust per-patient concordance signal (Platt-calibrated balanced p = 1.02e-09) following direction anchor training. Gate development resolves three sequential failure modes — global threshold shift, direction instability, and calibration overfitting — documented as a reproducible engineering record. The empirical flagging threshold on calibrated alpha is 0.75–0.80; both flagship borderline patients (TCGA-BH-A0HK, TCGA-BH-A0HW) exceed this threshold and are automatically routed to pathologist review.
  • Top-1%-Mass Concentration: a normalization-independent interpretability metric that quantifies attentional focus consistently across any slide size, revealing phenotypic separation between PR- and PR+ patients (p = 0.018) that entropy-based analysis entirely suppresses (p = 0.401).

3 The Solution

3.1 Asymmetric Cross-Attention Architecture

The approach treats the patient’s transcriptomic profile as a query and the slide as the evidence being searched. Patch-level features are extracted using the CHIEF ViT-B encoder [6], pretrained on 60,530 whole-slide images spanning 19 anatomic sites across 14 study cohorts, including TCGA. Breast tissue is explicitly represented in this pretraining distribution, supporting the use of frozen 768-dimensional embeddings as a reliable visual feature extractor for TCGA-BRCA without dataset-specific fine-tuning. TCGA slides appear in the CHIEF pretraining corpus; however, CHIEF is used exclusively as a frozen feature extractor with no weight updates during training or fine-tuning on TCGA-BRCA. The cross-attention, gating, and classification weights are trained solely on the 760-patient training split with no access to held-out cohort labels, preserving the integrity of the 191-patient evaluation. The genomic RNA-Seq vector is compressed from 20,513 dimensions to 512 through a linear bottleneck with LayerNorm and GELU, forming a single query token Q, one vector per patient representing the entire transcriptomic profile. In parallel, each of the N patch embeddings is projected from 768 to 512 dimensions, forming keys K and values V. Q identifies which patches are relevant via K, and V provides the content that gets retrieved. With no positional encoding applied, the permutation invariance of the unordered patch bag is preserved.

The query token attends over all N patches simultaneously across 8 attention heads:

\text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) V

The attention computation uses 8 heads with d_k = 64 dimensions per head, derived by dividing the shared embedding dimension by the number of heads (512 ÷ 8 = 64). Each head runs its own independent attention computation in a 64-dimensional subspace, allowing the model to jointly attend to different aspects of the tissue simultaneously. The outputs of all 8 heads are concatenated back to 512 dimensions (8 × 64 = 512) before passing downstream. The scaling factor \sqrt{d_k} in the denominator prevents dot products from growing too large, which would push the softmax into regions with near-zero gradients and slow learning.

Because there is only one query token, the attention score matrix has shape (1 × N) rather than (N × N) for symmetric self-attention, giving linear rather than quadratic cost. At N = 39,052 patches, symmetric self-attention would require approximately 1.53 billion pairwise comparisons per forward pass; the asymmetric design requires only 39,052, a difference that determines whether the model fits on a standard workstation. The output is a single 512-dimensional context vector c representing the entire slide, weighted by the genomic query. This vector does not summarize the slide uniformly; it encodes the visual appearance of tissue regions the genomic profile deemed most diagnostically relevant.

3.2 Gated-Fusion Mechanism

Following cross-attention, the context vector c and query token Q are passed through a confidence-aware gating layer before the classification head, producing a scalar α ∈ (0, 1):

\alpha = \sigma\!\left(W_g \cdot [\mathbf{c} \Vert \mathbf{Q}] + b_g\right), \qquad \text{gated} = \alpha \cdot \mathbf{c}

The multiplication α · c is uniform scaling; every dimension of the 512-dimensional context vector is multiplied by the same scalar α.

The gating layer adds exactly 1,025 parameters: Wg contributes 1,024 weights (projecting the concatenated 1,024-dimensional vector to a scalar via a dot product) and bg contributes a single bias term. This represents 0.009% of the total 12,082,178 parameter count. The gating layer is designed for modular integration; gate parameters are initialized to neutral (α = 0.5) to preserve the learned cross-attention representation during v2 integration. Gate training results across six sessions are reported in Section 4.4. The final configuration — direction anchor loss with Platt calibration — produces a directionally stable, class-imbalance-robust per-patient concordance signal with an empirical flagging threshold of approximately 0.75–0.80 on calibrated alpha.

The gate functions as a Cross-Modal Reliability Estimator: it asks whether the genomic and visual streams tell the same story. When aligned, α → 1, retaining the full cross-attention signal. When discordant, as in cases near the IHC positivity threshold, a low calibrated α suppresses the visual contribution and triggers escalation to pathologist review. The gating layer operates on the aggregated 512-dimensional token rather than patch-level representations, preserving O(N) complexity while generating a reliability flag routing discordant cases to pathologist review.

NoteDesign Trade-off: Why a Gated Linear Projection?

Three candidate designs were evaluated against two constraints: O(N) complexity preservation and negligible parameter overhead.

  • Complex cross-modal attention refinement: a second cross-attention pass over N patches violates both constraints.
  • Simple concatenation: passing [c ‖ Q] directly to the classifier satisfies both constraints but combines the two modalities without assessing their agreement, producing no per-patient uncertainty signal.
  • Gated linear projection (chosen): α = σ(Wg · [c ‖ Q]) adds 1,025 parameters, preserves O(N), and produces an explicit per-patient confidence score.

3.3 The Top-1%-Mass Concentration Metric

The softmax in the attention computation produces a weight distribution w = (w₁, w₂, …, wₙ) over all N patches before aggregation into the context vector c. Each wᵢ reflects how much the genomic query attended to patch i. These weights are extracted at inference time to compute the Top-1%-Mass Concentration metric, which measures how concentrated the attention distribution is across the slide.

For each patient, attention weights are sorted in descending order and the top k = max(1, ⌊N × 0.01⌋) weights are identified — the 1% of patches the model attended to most. Top-1%-Mass Concentration is the fraction of total attention mass those top patches carry:

\text{Top-1\%-Mass} = \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k} w_{\sigma(i)}}{\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i}

The numerator sums the top k attention weights; the denominator normalizes by total attention mass across all N patches, making the metric directly comparable across patients regardless of slide size.

Interpreting the value is straightforward. For a perfectly uniform attention distribution, every patch receives equal weight and the metric equals exactly 0.01, the theoretical minimum. Values above this baseline indicate that attention is concentrated on a small number of patches rather than surveying the slide broadly. In this cohort, both PR- (mean 0.0888) and PR+ (mean 0.0705) patients show attention well above the uniform baseline, meaning the model attends focally in both groups. The key finding is that PR- patients show significantly higher concentration than PR+ patients (Welch t-test, p = 0.018), consistent with the discrete focal abnormalities characteristic of hormone receptor-negative disease. The difference in attentional behavior between PR- and PR+ patients is shown in Figure 2.

3.4 Why Cross-Modal Alignment Matters

The asymmetric cross-attention mechanism routes the genomic query vector g to search over visual patch embeddings v. For the gate to learn a semantically meaningful concordance signal, v and g must occupy a shared representational space where cosine similarity is informative. Under the current CHIEF ViT-B encoder—pretrained on pathology data without transcriptomic supervision—cosine similarity between v and g across the 191-patient held-out cohort is near-zero and negative (mean = -0.032, std = 0.039, range −0.106 to 0.085). The two streams are effectively orthogonal at the point of fusion.

This near-orthogonality has two consequences. First, the direction anchor loss:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{anchor}} = \text{MSE}\!\left(\alpha,\; \sigma(\text{cos}(v, g) \times 20)\right)

converges to anchor_target_mean = 0.359 rather than 0.5, consistent with the negative mean cosine similarity (sigmoid(-0.032 × 20) ≈ 0.345, close to the observed target mean). Second, the direction of the signal is sensitive to the sign of small cosine fluctuations, producing direction instability across training regimes without the anchor loss. Both consequences trace to the same root cause: an encoder not pretrained for cross-modal alignment.

Transcriptomics-guided slide representation learning — exemplified by TITAN [5] and RNAPath — addresses this at the encoder level by pretraining patch embeddings to align with RNA-Seq profiles. A TGSR encoder would produce v and g vectors with meaningfully higher cosine similarity, centering the anchor target distribution near 0.5 and stabilizing gate direction across training regimes without architectural changes to the fusion layer.

TipWhat This Work Contributes vs What TGSR Contributes

TGSR encoders solve the representation alignment prerequisite. The contributions of this work — asymmetric cross-attention at O(N) cost, the Cross-Modal Reliability Estimator, and the Top-1%-Mass Concentration metric — operate on top of that prerequisite and remain valid regardless of which encoder supplies v. Replacing CHIEF ViT-B with a TGSR encoder is an architectural upgrade expected to improve gate calibration; it is not a replacement of the fusion framework.

4.4 Architectural Evolution: Cross-Attention vs. Gated-Fusion

The central design question posed by the v1 system was not whether discriminative performance could be improved—the 191-patient cohort is insufficient to resolve sub-percent architectural deltas—but whether the system could surface modality-specific uncertainty quantification for cases where the two streams are in tension. Standard cross-attention produces a single scalar output with no mechanism for decomposing confidence across modalities.

Gate development required six training sessions and two calibration attempts to resolve three distinct failure modes: global threshold shift, direction instability, and calibration overfitting. The final configuration — direction anchor loss with sharpening factor 20, variance penalty λ = 0.001, Platt scaling calibration — produces a statistically robust, class-imbalance-resistant per-patient signal.

The direction flip between e2e (positive, PR+ higher raw α) and anchored (negative, PR- higher raw α) checkpoints traces to a sign difference in cosine similarity between independently-trained backbones: e2e retrains cross_attention and genomic_projector jointly with the gate, converging to mean cosine similarity +0.063 (std = 0.047), while the staged-to-anchored lineage freezes a backbone (inherited from the original two-stage training) with mean cosine similarity -0.032 (std = 0.039). Both are near-zero in magnitude, but the raw-alpha direction is determined by which backbone’s sign the checkpoint inherited, not by random per-run fluctuation. Platt calibration resolves this at the signal level regardless of the raw checkpoint’s direction: because it fits calibrated alpha directly against the PR+ label, the calibrated signal is by construction oriented so that higher calibrated alpha tracks PR+ status (positive direction) — the anchored-epoch-9-Platt row shows this alignment, and the raw direction underneath it remains negative. This is an encoder alignment problem, not a fusion architecture problem, and is the primary motivation for replacing CHIEF ViT-B with a transcriptomically-guided encoder in v3 (Section 3.4).

NoteWhat Is and Is Not Solved

The gate mechanism produces a statistically real, class-imbalance-robust, directionally stable per-patient concordance signal following anchor loss training and Platt calibration. The empirical flagging threshold is approximately 0.75–0.80 on calibrated alpha, reflecting the shifted anchor target distribution. The root cause — near-orthogonal visual and genomic embeddings from an encoder not pretrained for cross-modal alignment — is documented in Section 3.4 and prescribed for resolution in v3 via a transcriptomically-guided encoder.

WarningGate Direction Is Anchored, Not Intrinsic

The negative raw-alpha direction (PR- higher raw α) is consistent across all epochs of the final training run but reflects the sign of the cohort’s cosine similarity distribution rather than an architectural invariant. Replacing CHIEF ViT-B with a TGSR encoder is expected to center the anchor target distribution at 0.5 and stabilize direction intrinsically, without dependence on post-hoc calibration.

5 Clinical Insight

5.1 The Focal vs. Distributed Attention Finding

PR- patients exhibit higher Top-1%-Mass Concentration (0.0888 vs 0.0705, p = 0.018, held-out cohort), meaning the model allocates a larger fraction of attention mass onto a smaller fraction of patches for hormone receptor-negative slides. This is consistent with known histopathology: PR- tumors are characterized by focal architectural abnormalities — mitotic figures, nuclear pleomorphism, geographic necrosis, pushing borders — that represent discrete, spatially concentrated events [7]. PR+ tumors exhibit the diffuse, organized glandular architecture of luminal disease, where relevant features are distributed across a larger tissue area.

The model has learned to mirror this distinction without any supervision on which regions to attend to.

TipKey Takeaway for Diagnostics

The direction of the difference is the opposite of what naive entropy analysis suggested. Under the confounded metric, PR- patients appeared more diffuse. Under the normalized metric, they are significantly more focal. A diagnostic tool built on entropy would encode the wrong biology.

5.2 Implications for Borderline IHC Cases

Two validation patients — TCGA-BH-A0HK and TCGA-BH-A0HW — carry model probabilities above 0.79 despite PR-negative IHC designation. These are not straightforward errors: IHC-negative cases near the positivity threshold are known to exhibit partial or heterogeneous receptor expression that the binary read-out suppresses. The clinical question is not whether the model is wrong — it may not be — but whether it can differentiate between equally high-confidence predictions that rest on different evidential foundations.

The v1 architecture cannot. Both patients receive P(PR+) ≈ 0.80 with no mechanism to distinguish whether confidence is supported by both modalities or driven by one against the other. The v2 Cross-Modal Reliability Estimator, following direction anchor training and Platt calibration, is the direct architectural response.

WarningNote on Gate Values

The base checkpoint (best_model.pt) carries no trained gate weights; α = 0.5000 for all patients reflects correct neutral initialization. Calibrated alpha values below are from the anchored epoch 9 checkpoint with Platt calibration and represent real, trained gate outputs.

Both patients show high calibrated alpha (0.913, 0.854) despite PR-negative IHC designation. This is not a failure of the gate — it is the gate functioning as designed. High calibrated alpha indicates the visual and genomic streams are in agreement; the discordance is between the multimodal model and the binary IHC read-out. Both patients are precisely the cases the system was designed to flag: high-confidence multimodal evidence pointing toward PR+ against a negative IHC label near the positivity threshold, warranting escalation for quantitative H-score or Allred score re-evaluation.

NoteClinical Interpretation of Calibrated Alpha

The empirical flagging threshold on calibrated alpha is approximately 0.75–0.80, derived from the Platt-calibrated distribution on the training cohort. Patients with calibrated α > 0.80 and P(PR+) > 0.60 represent the highest-priority escalation candidates — cases where both modalities agree on a PR+ phenotype against a negative IHC label. TCGA-BH-A0HW meets this criterion in full (calibrated α = 0.913, P(PR+) = 0.689). TCGA-BH-A0HK clears the calibrated-alpha threshold (0.854) but its P(PR+) = 0.442 falls short of the 0.60 bar — illustrating that a high concordance signal alone does not guarantee independent classifier confidence. Both would still be routed to pathologist review under the v2 triage protocol on the calibrated-alpha threshold alone.

NoteFuture Validation Target

The gate produces a statistically real, class-imbalance-robust per-patient signal under both robust training regimes. The two prescribed next steps are: (1) replacing CHIEF ViT-B with a TGSR encoder to center the anchor target distribution and stabilize gate direction intrinsically; and (2) verifying that concordant cases (calibrated α > 0.80) and discordant cases (calibrated α < 0.80) show a separable distribution of quantitative H-scores among borderline IHC patients.

5.3 Validating Interpretability via Clinical Correlation

The model output is a diagnostic hypothesis grounded in joint transcriptomic-morphological evidence, not a definitive classification. When model confidence diverges from binary IHC read-out, the appropriate clinical response is escalation for quantitative H-score or Allred score re-evaluation, using the spotlight maps in Figure 5 to identify tile regions of highest attentional weight.

6 Conclusion

This work demonstrates that genomic-conditioned visual search is a principled and effective approach to multimodal fusion in computational pathology, and that architectural efficiency and biological interpretability are mutually reinforcing rather than competing goals. By treating the patient’s transcriptomic profile as a query token that interrogates the slide rather than summarizing it, the asymmetric cross-attention mechanism achieves an ROC-AUC of 0.8482 and PR-AUC of 0.9054 on 951 TCGA-BRCA patients, outperforming unimodal and late-fusion baselines while requiring no positional encoding, pathway annotation, or pre-aggregation.

The Cross-Modal Reliability Estimator extends this into a clinically actionable system that recognizes its own limitations — routing cases where genomic and imaging evidence are weakly aligned to pathologist review rather than consuming them as high-confidence binary predictions. Gate development required six training sessions and two calibration attempts to resolve three sequential failure modes: global threshold shift, direction instability, and calibration overfitting. The final anchored checkpoint with Platt calibration produces a directionally stable, class-imbalance-robust per-patient concordance signal (balanced p = 1.02e-09), with both flagship borderline patients automatically routed to pathologist review under the empirical flagging threshold.

The Top-1%-Mass Concentration metric demonstrates that failures in interpretability are often errors in measurement rather than architecture. Shannon entropy yields p = 0.401 on the held-out cohort; the normalization-corrected metric recovers p = 0.018 on the same data, with PR- patients showing significantly more focal attention patterns than PR+ patients — consistent with known histopathology and confirming the model learns medical reality rather than statistical artifacts. It is the measurement instrument, not the model, that determines whether the signal is recoverable.

Taken together, these contributions establish a blueprint for clinically actionable AI in pathology: computationally scalable, transparent in its reasoning, and designed to augment rather than replace the judgment of the human pathologist.

7 Future Work

Gate direction stabilization via transcriptomically-guided encoder. Six training sessions and two calibration attempts resolved three sequential gate failure modes: global threshold shift, direction instability, and calibration overfitting. The final anchored checkpoint with Platt calibration produces a directionally stable, class-imbalance-robust signal (balanced p = 1.02e-09). The remaining open problem is that gate direction reflects the sign of the cohort’s cosine similarity distribution rather than an architectural invariant, because CHIEF ViT-B visual embeddings and RNA-Seq projections are nearly orthogonal (cosine similarity mean = -0.032). The prescribed next step is replacing CHIEF ViT-B with a transcriptomically-guided encoder such as TITAN [5] or RNAPath, which aligns patch-level visual features with RNA-Seq profiles during pretraining. This is an encoder swap, not an architectural change — the asymmetric cross-attention, gate, and Top-1%-Mass Concentration metric are encoder-agnostic.

Expanded cohort size and generalization. The 191-patient held-out cohort is insufficient to detect small but meaningful differences between model versions with statistical confidence, or to characterize cross-site generalization. Validation on an independent external cohort, targeting a minimum of 500 held-out patients, is the prescribed next step.

Fail-Safe Diagnostic Mode validation. When RNA-Seq data is unavailable the system falls back to a unimodal MIL classifier on CHIEF ViT-B embeddings alone. Independent benchmarking across cohorts and acquisition platforms is required to confirm the quantified AUC reduction relative to the bimodal system is stable and acceptable for clinical deployment.

Lessons Learned

Regularization is an architectural constraint, not a tuning hyperparameter. A training AUC of 0.9912 against a validation AUC of 0.6240 is evidence that the model memorizes labels without learning biology. A 0.37-point overfit gap is the specification for the regularization requirement — enforce it with the same rigor as architecture, not as a post-hoc correction.

Metric validation is a primary engineering task. Shannon entropy applied to variable-length attention distributions measures a mixture of attentional focus and slide size. The transition from p = 0.401 to p = 0.018 via a single normalization step confirms the measurement instrument was the bottleneck — not the model, not the biology. Any WSI interpretability metric should be verified for input-size invariance before reporting group-level conclusions.

Statistical significance on a held-out cohort is necessary but not sufficient for a clinically deployable signal. The staged checkpoint produced p = 0.030 on the full cohort but did not survive class balancing (balanced p = 0.067). The same confound instinct that caught the Shannon entropy slide-size problem applies here: a significant result that depends on cohort imbalance is measuring the imbalance, not the biology. Always verify held-out significance on a balanced subsample before reporting a gate signal as robust.

Representational prerequisites determine fusion ceiling. The gate direction instability across training regimes traces to a single root cause: CHIEF ViT-B patch embeddings and RNA-Seq projections are nearly orthogonal (cosine similarity mean = -0.032) because the encoder was not pretrained for cross-modal alignment. The anchor loss compensates but cannot fully correct for an encoder that wasn’t trained to express molecular phenotype in its patch representations. Verify that input representations are alignable before designing a fusion mechanism. If cross-modal cosine similarity is uniformly near zero on a held-out cohort, the fusion layer is operating without a shared semantic foundation and any concordance signal it learns will require post-hoc calibration to be clinically usable.

Cross-modal conditioning upstream of compression is qualitatively different from late fusion. The +0.0801 AUC margin is structural, not parametric — both architectures used identical inputs under identical conditions. The design question is not “at which layer do we fuse?” but “at which stage does one modality have decision-relevant information about the other?” — and fuse there.

Architectural maturity means knowing when not to optimize for AUC. The Cross-Modal Reliability Estimator improved AUC by +0.0056 incidentally — its primary objective was a per-patient concordance signal, not discriminative performance. A system whose only output is a discriminative score has a fixed ceiling on clinical utility. Designing for the failure modes that high AUC conceals is what makes a system safe for clinical adoption.

Engineering Notes

The following documents implementation decisions, known constraints, and resilience design. These are engineering properties of the current system, not aspirational claims.

NoteReproducibility

All discriminative results derive from a single best-checkpoint model — Cross-Attention v1 — trained for 10 epochs on 760 patients (stratified 80/20 split of 951 total, random_state=42), Adam optimizer, lr = 1 × 10⁻⁴, weight decay = 1 × 10⁻⁴, batch size 4, validated on a held-out 191-patient cohort with no data leakage. The Cross-Modal Reliability Estimator (v2) is trained separately: gate weights initialized to zero, direction anchor loss (λ_anchor = 0.10, sharpening factor 20), variance penalty (λ_var = 0.001), 10 epochs, best checkpoint at epoch 9 (val_auc = 0.8482). Platt calibration fitted on training-set alpha values; calibrator saved to checkpoints/platt_calibrator_anchored.pkl.

Parameter count: 12,082,178 · Attention heads: 8 (dk = 64) · Patch embedding: CHIEF ViT-B (Dearcat/CPathPatchFeature, dim = 768) · Genomic features: 20,513 RNA-Seq RSEM (cBioPortal) · Patch count range: 960–39,052

Determinism-First Regularization. Dropout is excluded from the genomic projector and the Cross-Modal Reliability Estimator. Stochastic variation in either pathway would produce irreproducible spotlight maps and triage signals across runs on the same patient. Regularization is achieved instead through LayerNorm and weight decay (λ = 1 × 10⁻⁴). Dropout is retained post-fusion (rate 0.1) where stochasticity carries no interpretability cost.

7.1 Limitations

WarningKnown Constraints

The following are structural properties of the current system, not incidental weaknesses.

Data generalization. Trained and validated on a single TCGA-BRCA cohort; out-of-distribution performance across acquisition platforms and staining protocols is uncharacterized.

Attention as correlate, not explanation. Top-1%-Mass Concentration measures where the model concentrates capacity, not why. Tissue processing artifacts can dominate attention weight without biological basis; findings are correlational signals warranting expert annotation validation.

Permutation invariance. Spatial relationships between tiles are not captured. For PR status classification this is likely modest; for grading or treatment response prediction it would be considerably more consequential.

Visual-molecular representation alignment. The CHIEF ViT-B encoder was pretrained on pathology data without transcriptomic supervision, producing visual embeddings v and genomic embeddings g that are nearly orthogonal at inference time (cosine similarity mean = -0.032, std = 0.039 across the 191-patient held-out cohort). This near-orthogonality limits anchor loss gradient and is the root cause of gate direction sensitivity across training regimes. The prescribed architectural upgrade for v3 is a transcriptomically-guided encoder such as TITAN [5] or RNAPath. This is an encoder swap, not an architectural change — the asymmetric cross-attention, gate, and Top-1%-Mass Concentration metric are encoder-agnostic.

Clinical throughput. RNA-Seq turnaround of 5–10 business days confines the system to retrospective or prospective study contexts. Outputs in P(PR+) ∈ [0.40, 0.75] should be flagged for specialist review, not treated as actionable binary predictions.

7.2 System Resilience

If RNA-Seq data is unavailable, the system produces no output. A null output in a clinical setting is not a safe state. A Fail-Safe Diagnostic Mode — a unimodal MIL classifier on CHIEF ViT-B embeddings alone, equivalent to the CLAM-SB baseline (AUC 0.6926 on the held-out cohort) — provides a quantified fallback with a 15.6-point AUC reduction preferable to silence.

WarningResilience Is Not Optional for Clinical Deployment

The system must guarantee output at every input state: bimodal inference when both modalities are present, Fail-Safe Mode when genomic data is absent, and a structured escalation alert when neither is operable. A null output without a logged, actionable escalation is a system failure.
