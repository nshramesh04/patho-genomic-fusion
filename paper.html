Asymmetric Cross-Attention Networks for Multimodal Integration in Pathology

Genomic-Conditioned Visual Search for PR-Status Classification in Breast Cancer

Author
Affiliation

Nisha Ramesh

Independent Research

Published

July 24, 2026

Abstract

Accurate progesterone receptor (PR) status is a critical determinant of endocrine therapy eligibility in breast cancer, yet standard immunohistochemistry (IHC) exhibits substantial inter-pathologist variability in diagnostically ambiguous cases. We present a multimodal clinical decision-support system that integrates RNA-Seq transcriptomic profiles with whole-slide imaging (WSI) to improve PR status prediction. To address the mismatch between molecular profiles and variable-length WSI representations, we introduce an asymmetric cross-attention mechanism in which the patient’s transcriptomic profile guides visual evidence retrieval from the tissue image with linear computational complexity. A Cross-Modal Reliability Estimator provides a calibrated per-patient confidence score to identify cases requiring additional pathologist review. A normalization-corrected Top-1%-Mass Concentration metric quantifies attentional focus consistently across slides of any size.

Using the TCGA-BRCA cohort of 951 patients with matched molecular and imaging data, the model achieved an ROC-AUC of 0.8482 and PR-AUC of 0.9054. The reliability estimator produces a statistically robust per-patient confidence score, enabling automated escalation of discordant cases to pathologist review. Analysis of attention patterns revealed significant phenotypic separation between PR-positive and PR-negative tumors (p = 0.018), suggesting the model captures spatial patterns associated with PR phenotype.

Together, these findings demonstrate that genomic-conditioned visual search is an effective framework for multimodal computational pathology. The results further show that the measurement instrument, not the model, determines whether biological signal is recoverable.

Keywords

progesterone receptor status, cross-attention, whole-slide imaging, computational pathology, uncertainty quantification, RNA-Seq

1 The Challenge

1.1 Clinical Necessity

PR status determines whether a breast cancer patient is a candidate for endocrine therapies such as tamoxifen and aromatase inhibitors. A misclassification in either direction carries direct clinical cost: false positives expose patients to ineffective hormonal therapy, while false negatives withhold potentially curative treatment. The current gold standard, IHC staining of biopsy tissue, is subject to significant inter-pathologist disagreement, particularly in borderline cases near the positivity threshold.

1.2 The Structural Incompatibility Problem

The core engineering challenge is data structure, not modeling capacity. A transcriptomic profile is a fixed-length vector occupying an identical coordinate space across all patients. A digitized whole-slide image yields a variable-length, unordered patch bag whose size varies by more than an order of magnitude depending on tissue area and scanner footprint. Standard fusion architectures resolve this by either compressing the patch bag to a fixed summary, discarding spatial specificity, or imposing positional encodings tied to scanner trajectory rather than tissue biology. Both approaches fail to generalize across acquisition platforms.

1.3 The Slide-Size Confounder

A third problem emerges not in the fusion architecture but in evaluating it. Shannon entropy, the intuitive metric for attentional focus, is mathematically bounded above by log N. A patient with a large slide can appear to have more diffuse attention than a patient with an identical distribution on a small slide, purely as a function of scanner footprint, not biology. Any interpretability metric applied to variable-length attention distributions must be normalized for input size before group-level conclusions can be drawn.

2 Related Work

Prior approaches address parts of these challenges but each leaves a critical gap.

Attention-based MIL [1], [2] operates within the visual modality alone, sidestepping the structural incompatibility problem by ignoring genomics entirely.

Multimodal fusion [3] incorporates genomics but defers the incompatibility rather than resolving it. Fusion occurs after the slide has already been compressed to a fixed-size summary, so the genomic signal conditions how an already-compressed visual summary is weighted, not which tissue evidence is gathered.

Cross-modal transformers [4] bring genomic conditioning upstream of compression but require predefined gene-pathway annotations and scanner-tied positional encodings. The slide-size confounder is unaddressed; no normalization-independent interpretability metric is proposed.

None of the above provides a mechanism for flagging cases where genomic and imaging evidence are in tension, which is precisely where binary IHC classification is least reliable.

3 Contributions

This work introduces three components that together resolve the structural, clinical triage, and interpretability gaps identified above:

Asymmetric cross-attention fusion treats the patient’s transcriptomic profile as a query token that searches the entire slide for diagnostically relevant tissue at linear computational cost, without positional encoding, pathway annotation, or pre-aggregation.

Cross-Modal Reliability Estimator is a lightweight gated fusion layer that produces a scalar confidence score reflecting cross-modal agreement between genomic and imaging evidence. Discordant cases are flagged for pathologist review alongside the model prediction, extending the system beyond a discriminative score into a clinically actionable triage tool.

Top-1%-Mass Concentration is a normalization-independent interpretability metric that quantifies attentional focus across any slide size. It recovers statistically significant phenotypic separation between PR- and PR+ patients that entropy-based analysis fails to recover on the same cohort, demonstrating that the measurement instrument rather than the model was the bottleneck.

4 Methods

4.1 Asymmetric Cross-Attention Architecture

The approach treats the patient’s transcriptomic profile as a query and the slide as the evidence being searched. Patch-level features come from the frozen CHIEF ViT-B encoder [5]. The genomic RNA-Seq vector is compressed from 20,513 dimensions to 512, forming a single query token Q. Each of the N patch embeddings is projected from 768 to 512 dimensions, forming keys K and values V. No positional encoding is applied, preserving permutation invariance of the unordered patch bag. Dimensionality flow through each component is summarized in Table 1.

The query token attends over all N patch embeddings simultaneously:

\text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) V

Because Q has shape (1 × N) rather than (N × N), attention cost scales linearly with slide size rather than quadratically. The output, denoted c, encodes the visual appearance of tissue regions the genomic profile deemed most diagnostically relevant.

4.2 Gated-Fusion Mechanism

The Gated-Fusion layer estimates cross-modal agreement between the genomic and visual streams on a per-patient basis. The context vector c and query token Q are concatenated and passed through a single linear projection with Sigmoid, producing a scalar confidence score α ∈ (0, 1):

\alpha = \sigma\!\left(W_g \cdot [\mathbf{c} \Vert \mathbf{Q}] + b_g\right), \qquad \text{gated} = \alpha \cdot \mathbf{c}

When aligned, α → 1, retaining the full cross-attention signal. When discordant, a low α suppresses the visual contribution and triggers escalation to pathologist review following Platt calibration of the gate output.

The overall architecture is shown in Figure 1.

4.3 The Top-1%-Mass Concentration Metric

The attention computation produces a weight distribution over all N patches, extracted at inference time to quantify attentional focus. For each patient, the top 1% of patches by attention weight are identified; Top-1%-Mass Concentration is the fraction of total attention mass those patches carry:

\text{Top-1\%-Mass} = \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k} w_{\sigma(i)}}{\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i}

The denominator normalizes by total attention mass, making the metric directly comparable across patients regardless of slide size. PR- patients show significantly higher concentration than PR+ patients (p = 0.018), consistent with the focal architectural abnormalities characteristic of hormone receptor-negative disease, shown in Figure 2.

5 Performance Evaluation

This section reports discriminative performance against unimodal and multimodal baselines, interpretability analysis, gate performance, and known constraints on generalizability.

5.1 Model Comparison

Four models are compared: the proposed Cross-Attention + Gate system, Late Fusion, ABMIL, and CLAM-SB. The model was evaluated on a stratified held-out cohort of 191 patients (80/20 split of 951 total), with 128 PR+ and 63 PR- cases reflecting the 2:1 class imbalance of the full dataset. All results derive from inference on trained checkpoints under identical conditions. Weight decay regularization is load-bearing for the base classifier; an unregularized ablation produced a 0.37-point overfit gap (training AUC 0.9912, validation AUC 0.6240).

Results resolve into two performance bands. Unimodal systems plateau near AUC 0.69 to 0.71, unable to distinguish PR+ from PR- slides without transcriptomic state. Late Fusion closes part of the remaining gap by incorporating genomics, but fusion occurs after compression. The cross-attention model applies genomics at the search stage, before compression, accounting for a +0.0801 AUC margin over Late Fusion and +0.1388 over the best unimodal baseline, summarized in Table 4. ROC and Precision-Recall curves are shown in Figure 3.

5.2 Interpretability

Analysis of attention patterns using the Top-1%-Mass Concentration metric reveals significantly higher attentional focus in PR- patients than PR+ patients (p = 0.018), consistent with the focal architectural abnormalities characteristic of hormone receptor-negative disease (Figure 4). The entropy-based metric yields p = 0.401 on the same data, confirming the signal was suppressed by the slide-size confounder rather than absent from the model.

5.3 Gate Performance

Training the Cross-Modal Reliability Estimator requires the gate to learn a per-patient signal rather than a uniform shift across the cohort. Standard training with BCE loss alone produced global threshold collapse; the gate shifted α uniformly rather than discriminating between patients. A direction anchor loss was introduced to resolve this:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{anchor}} = \text{MSE}\!\left(\alpha,\; \sigma(s \cdot \text{cos}(v, g))\right)

where s is a sharpening factor that scales cosine similarity before the sigmoid, spreading anchor targets away from 0.5. This ties α directly to cosine similarity between the visual embedding v and genomic query g: high α indicates the two streams are in agreement, low α indicates discordance. In this cohort, CHIEF ViT-B visual embeddings and RNA-Seq projections are nearly orthogonal (cosine similarity mean = -0.032), so s = 20 was required to produce meaningful anchor targets.

The Cross-Modal Reliability Estimator produces a directionally stable, class-imbalance-robust per-patient concordance signal (balanced p = 1.02e-09, verified on a class-balanced subsample to confirm the signal is not imbalance-driven) following anchor loss training. The threshold is applied to the Platt-calibrated output, not the raw gate value; raw alpha ranges from 0.048 to 0.488, entirely below the nominal 0.5 boundary. Platt calibration rescales the distribution to produce a clinically usable signal, with an empirical flagging threshold of approximately 0.75 to 0.80 on calibrated alpha.

5.4 Known Constraints

Training and validation are confined to a single TCGA-BRCA cohort; out-of-distribution performance is uncharacterized. Top-1%-Mass Concentration measures where the model concentrates capacity, not why; tissue processing artifacts can dominate attention weight without biological basis. Spatial relationships between tiles are not captured, which is acceptable for PR-status classification but would be consequential for grading or treatment response prediction.

6 Clinical Insight

This section examines what the model has learned about tissue biology, how the gate signal behaves on diagnostically ambiguous cases, and what the attention patterns reveal about PR phenotype. The findings ground the system’s outputs in known histopathology and define the conditions under which automated escalation to pathologist review is triggered.

6.1 Attention Patterns and PR Phenotype

The p = 0.018 finding reported in Section 5.2 reflects a genuine biological distinction: PR- patients exhibit higher Top-1%-Mass Concentration than PR+ patients (0.0888 vs 0.0705), consistent with the discrete, spatially concentrated abnormalities characteristic of hormone receptor-negative disease — mitotic figures, geographic necrosis, and pushing borders [6] — while PR+ tumors exhibit the diffuse, organized glandular architecture of luminal disease. The model has learned to mirror this distinction without any supervision on which regions to attend to. Attention spotlight maps confirming this spatial separation are shown in Figure 5.

6.2 Borderline IHC Cases

The Cross-Modal Reliability Estimator is designed to flag cases where genomic and imaging evidence disagree, enabling selective escalation to pathologist review. On the 191-patient held-out cohort, the gate demonstrates the intended directional behavior: among concordant cases (calibrated α > 0.75), 83.1% are PR+; among flagged cases (α < 0.75), 67.2% are PR-, as shown in Table 5.

The gate correctly separates classes in both directions, confirming the mechanism works as designed. The current escalation rate of 31.9% reflects the embedding alignment gap between CHIEF ViT-B and RNA-Seq projections rather than a fundamental limitation of the gating approach. A transcriptomically-guided encoder is expected to sharpen the concordance signal and reduce the escalation rate to clinically actionable levels.

The 22 PR- patients in the concordant band represent a separate finding: cases where genomic and imaging evidence agree on a PR+ phenotype against the IHC label. These are potential IHC errors where the binary read-out may have suppressed partial or heterogeneous receptor expression near the positivity threshold, warranting quantitative H-score or Allred score re-evaluation.

7 Summary

This work demonstrates that genomic-conditioned visual search is a principled and effective approach to multimodal fusion in computational pathology. By treating the patient’s transcriptomic profile as a query token that interrogates the whole-slide image, the proposed asymmetric cross-attention mechanism achieves an ROC-AUC of 0.8482 and PR-AUC of 0.9054 on 951 TCGA-BRCA patients, outperforming unimodal and late-fusion baselines without requiring positional encoding, pathway annotation, or pre-aggregation.

The Cross-Modal Reliability Estimator demonstrates the intended directional behavior on the held-out cohort: concordant cases are enriched for PR+ patients (83.1%) and flagged cases are enriched for PR- patients (67.2%), confirming the mechanism works as designed. The proposed Top-1%-Mass Concentration metric reveals biologically meaningful attention patterns that conventional entropy measures fail to detect. Together, these results demonstrate that effective multimodal pathology systems require not only robust fusion architectures but also principled methods for uncertainty estimation and interpretability. The escalation rate is expected to improve when the current visual encoder is replaced with a transcriptomically-guided foundation model such as TITAN [7], which would strengthen cross-modal alignment and eliminate the dependence on post-hoc gate calibration.
